A growth plan is only useful when its assumptions connect to revenue.

Teams often plan traffic, pipeline, product adoption, and retention in separate documents. Each document can look reasonable while the full system does not add up. A growth model makes those dependencies visible.

## Start with the opening state

Record the latest complete month. Include the audience entering the funnel, the customers won, the customers retained, and recurring revenue. This is the base every forecast month inherits.

## Make timing explicit

Demand does not become revenue immediately. Account for the time between a visit, signup or qualified opportunity, and a purchase. A monthly model should carry conversions into the month when they actually happen.

## Connect every channel to the same system

Each channel needs a launch date, traffic or spend assumption, conversion rate, and customer value. Once launched, its customers and revenue should experience the same churn, expansion, and downgrade assumptions as the rest of the business.

## Use the model to choose the work

The goal is not a perfect prediction. The goal is to identify which assumption most constrains the revenue target, then choose the smallest useful change that can test it.
