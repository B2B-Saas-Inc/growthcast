# Human First Writing Standard

Use this standard for every GrowthCast article draft and edit. The author writes as a direct, experienced operator speaking to a smart peer. The copy should sound natural when read aloud.

## Hard rules

1. Do not use em dashes or double hyphens in article copy.
2. Use active voice. Name the person or group who acts.
3. Do not make an abstract idea such as strategy, clarity, growth, culture, trust, or momentum perform an action. Put people back in the sentence.
4. Address the reader as `you` when direct guidance helps.
5. Cut filler, corporate phrasing, stock transitions, familiar metaphors, clichés, and jargon.
6. Prefer short, common words. Give each sentence one main point when possible.
7. Use the same term for the same thing. Make every pronoun reference clear.
8. State instructions in the order the reader should follow them.
9. Keep uncertainty visible. Do not make an unsupported claim sound certain.

## Draft and edit process

Before drafting, name the human author, what the author cares about, one non-obvious view, and the default voice. For GrowthCast, use EJ White as the author and a direct, practical, conversational voice. Write one sentence that sounds specific to that author.

Draft roughly. Then edit structure before sentences. Avoid the same problem, advice, benefit, and call-to-action outline in every post. Mix observations, questions, short stories, instructions, and unresolved tradeoffs. Use headings only when readers need them. Vary paragraph and sentence length.

Run an agency pass. Ask who acts in every sentence. Recast sentences where an abstract noun performs the action.

Run an AI-signature pass. Remove mechanical precision, flat competence, rigid guidance, robotic formality, formulaic flow, overly tidy grammar, stock transitions, predictable structure, excessive headings, engineered connective tissue, and needless jargon.

Add at least one concrete observed detail to each major section. Include one unexpected sentence and one natural conversational moment in every article. Use contractions where they fit. A fragment is welcome when it adds useful emphasis.

Read the full draft aloud. Rewrite any section that sounds like a presentation or corporate memo. Break repeated sentence and paragraph patterns.

## Final review

- Article copy contains no em dash or double hyphen.
- People perform the actions.
- The writer addresses the reader where useful.
- No banned stock transition appears.
- Paragraph and sentence lengths vary.
- At least one fragment appears when the format allows it.
- The piece includes a concrete observed detail, a conversational moment, and a genuinely unexpected sentence.
- Technical terms use a plain definition when readers need one.
- The ending fits the idea instead of repeating a generic summary.
- The final read-aloud pass sounds like a person, not a corporate memo.

Run `node scripts/check-blog-writing.mjs` before approving or scheduling a post. The automated check enforces only rules that software can test reliably. The editor still owns voice, structure, agency, specificity, and the read-aloud review.
