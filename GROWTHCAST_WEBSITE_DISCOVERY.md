# GrowthCast Website Discovery Session

## Purpose

Use this document as a spoken-answer guide for a Wispr Flow session. The answers will provide the raw material for GrowthCast's positioning, site architecture, homepage, supporting pages, proof, and conversion journey.

This is a discovery document, not a copy draft. Speak naturally. Concrete examples are more useful than polished language.

## Confirmed direction

- **Brand:** GrowthCast
- **Category idea:** GTM Engineering for Growth
- **Best-fit company:** Series A or later, with product-market fit, traction, and a clear product vision, but without the repeatable systems needed to scale
- **Primary audiences:** CEOs and founders, VC partners, and private-equity partners
- **Operating philosophy:** Modified AAARRR model: Awareness, Acquisition, Activation, Revenue, Retention, Referral
- **Planning framework:** Current Demand / Future Demand
- **Capabilities informing the offer:** Fractional Growth Leadership, CRM Implementation, AI Enablement, Conversion Rate Optimization, Product Management, SEO/GEO/AEO, Lifecycle and Marketing Automation, Analytics, CRM, and Paid Media
- **Navigation constraint:** There will not be a Services tab. The capability list informs the overall GrowthCast vision rather than becoming a conventional agency service catalog.
- **Product location:** The forecast tool will move to **Resources > Tools > Forecast**.

## Writing standard for the final site

Use the website copywriting skill and ground each claim in a customer job, problem, objection, differentiator, or verified proof point.

Apply STE-100 principles where they improve clear public-facing copy:

- Use common, concrete words.
- Keep sentences short and direct.
- Express one main point per sentence.
- Keep instructions and calls to action simple.
- Avoid dense noun phrases, vague pronouns, and needless modifiers.
- Use the same term for the same idea.
- Prefer active voice.
- Explain any necessary technical term in plain English.

Apply George Orwell's six rules:

1. Never use a familiar metaphor, simile, or figure of speech.
2. Never use a long word when a short word will do.
3. Cut every word that does not add meaning.
4. Use active voice when possible.
5. Prefer everyday English to foreign phrases, scientific terms, and jargon.
6. Break a rule rather than write something ugly or unclear.

Additional constraints:

- Do not use em dashes or double hyphens.
- Do not invent claims, metrics, quotations, or proof.
- Do not use jargon to hide a weak or vague idea.
- Do not force strict controlled-English syntax when it makes persuasive copy stiff, unnatural, or less accurate.

## How to answer

1. Work through one group at a time.
2. Say the question number before answering it.
3. Use real examples, client language, numbers, objections, and stories whenever possible.
4. Say “unknown,” “needs verification,” or “cannot publish” rather than filling gaps.
5. Distinguish what you personally did from what the wider team accomplished.
6. Flag every company name, logo, quotation, or metric that requires publication permission.
